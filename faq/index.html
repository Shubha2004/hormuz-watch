Frequently asked questions

▸ Is the data on Hormuz Watch real-time?

No. Unless a panel is explicitly marked LIVE, all figures on this site are demonstration data — simulated or estimated values bundled with the app so you can explore the interface. Every panel shows its data quality and source. See the Methodology page for details.

▸ Why is the Strait of Hormuz strategically important?

The strait is the only sea passage from the Persian Gulf to the open ocean. A large share of globally traded crude oil and liquefied natural gas passes through it, so disruptions here move energy prices worldwide.

▸ What does "war-risk premium" mean?

When underwriters consider a region riskier, they charge an additional premium on hull and cargo insurance for transits. Higher premiums raise the delivered cost of oil and goods. Figures shown here are illustrative estimates, not quotes.

▸ What is AIS?

The Automatic Identification System broadcasts a vessel’s identity, position, course and speed. Public AIS aggregators are the usual source for vessel-tracking dashboards; this demo uses fictional vessels instead of real AIS data.

▸ What is a VLCC?

A Very Large Crude Carrier — a tanker class of roughly 200,000–320,000 deadweight tonnes, the workhorse of long-haul crude transport from the Gulf.

▸ How should I interpret the risk index?

As a demonstration composite only. In a production deployment it would blend incident frequency, insurance pricing and traffic anomalies. Here it is a static illustrative value.

▸ Can I use this data for trading or navigation decisions?

Absolutely not. All data here is simulated for demonstration. Never use it for commercial, navigational or safety-critical decisions.

▸ How do I connect real data sources?

See the Methodology page and .env.example. The provider layer is designed so real APIs (e.g. EIA for oil prices, AIS providers for vessels) can be plugged in behind the same interface.

Glossary

A B D E J K L P S T V W

A

AIS
Automatic Identification System — broadcasts vessel identity, position, course and speed.

B

Ballast
Seawater taken on for stability when a tanker sails without cargo.
Bunker fuel
Fuel oil consumed by ships; VLSFO is the common low-sulphur grade.

D

Deadweight tonnage (DWT)
Total weight a ship can safely carry, including cargo, fuel and stores.
Demurrage
Charges payable when a chartered vessel is delayed beyond agreed laytime.

E

Estimated time of arrival (ETA)
Projected arrival time at the next port or waypoint.

J

JKM
Japan-Korea Marker — the benchmark price for spot LNG in Northeast Asia.

K

Knot (kn)
Nautical mile per hour; the standard unit of vessel speed.

L

LNG
Liquefied natural gas — natural gas cooled to liquid for ocean transport.

P

P&I Club
Mutual insurance association covering shipowners’ third-party liabilities.

S

Strait of Hormuz
Narrow strait between Iran and Oman linking the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman.

T

TSS
Traffic Separation Scheme — charted lanes separating opposing traffic flows.

V

VLCC
Very Large Crude Carrier — tanker class of ~200,000–320,000 DWT.

W

War-risk premium
Additional insurance charge for transiting areas underwriters deem risky.
Worldscale (WS)
Index system for quoting tanker freight rates as a percentage of a nominal flat rate.
WTI
West Texas Intermediate — the US crude-oil benchmark grade.
